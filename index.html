Hindi LLaMA-Omni: a Hindi speech-to-speech assistant

October 2025 – October 2026 · Applied deep learning project with Prof. Mina Rezaei · code · model · dataset

1× A100 80 GB · 9.02 B parameters, 357 M trained · 35–40 hours of training

An audio chatbot for the Hindi language. The recipe has three main ingredients: a speech encoder (Whisper large-v3), a speech adaptor that learns the correspondence between speech tokens and text tokens, and a backbone language model (Llama 3.1 8B). The model is built on top of LLaMA-Omni.

  • Dataset. No spoken-Hindi instruction data existed, so I built Hindi-speech-instruct: 110,700 AI4Bharat Indic-Instruct conversations with every question synthesised to 16 kHz speech (158 GB, CC BY 4.0).
  • Training. Joint training from the English weights was unstable, so stage 1 teaches Hindi from 102k text conversations and stage 2 trains the speech projector on 49,787 spoken questions (160 hours). LoRA r = 128, α = 64, learning rate 1e-4 with cosine decay, bf16-mixed, about 29 GB of GPU memory per stage.
  • Results against the base model: IndicQA F1 34% → 68%, perplexity 13.2 → 7.7, MT-Bench-Hi 3.5 → 4.6 out of 10, IFEval-Hi 19.6% → 24.4%. Gains in question answering, writing and extraction; math and coding slightly worse.

Stable Diffusion from scratch

2025 · code

≈ 1.07 B parameters loaded · CLIP, VAE, U-Net, DDPM · 512² → 4×64×64 latents · 50 steps, CFG 7.5

Every module of Stable Diffusion v1.5 reimplemented in PyTorch: the CLIP text encoder, VAE encoder and decoder, U-Net with self- and cross-attention, and the DDPM sampler with the original 1,000-step schedule. Without the compute to train it, I wrote a converter that loads the official checkpoint into my own modules. Supports classifier-free guidance, image-to-image, and returning every denoising step.

Fourier epicycles from any image

2024 · live demo · code

Canny → nearest-neighbour ordering → parametric spline → FFT · up to 2,000 edge points · FastAPI, OpenCV, SciPy, JS canvas

Upload a picture and a chain of rotating circles redraws its outline. Edge points are ordered into a single path, smoothed with a spline, encoded as z = x + iy and Fourier transformed; the largest frequencies become the circles. Works well on cartoons and high-contrast images, less so on photographs.
