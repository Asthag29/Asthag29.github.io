Hindi LLaMA-Omni: a Hindi speech-to-speech assistant

October 2025 – October 2026 · Applied deep learning project with Prof. Mina Rezaei · code · model · dataset

1× A100 80 GB · 9.02 B parameters, 357 M trained · 35–40 hours of training

An audio chatbot for the Hindi language. The recipe has three main ingredients: a speech encoder (Whisper large-v3), a speech adaptor that learns the correspondence between speech tokens and text tokens, and a backbone language model (Llama 8B). The model is built on top of LLaMA-Omni.

  • Dataset. No spoken-Hindi instruction data existed, so I built Hindi-speech-instruct: 110,700 AI4Bharat Indic-Instruct conversations with every question synthesised to 16 kHz speech (158 GB, CC BY 4.0).
  • Training. Training was done in two stages: the first stage trains the LoRA adaptors on 102k Hindi text conversations, and the second stage trains the speech adaptor on 49,787 spoken questions (160 hours).
  • Results against the base model: IndicQA (question answering) F1 34% → 68%, perplexity 13.2 → 7.7; MT-Bench-Hi (conversation) 3.5 → 4.6 out of 10; IFEval-Hi (instruction following) 19.6% → 24.4%. Gains in question answering, writing and extraction; math and coding slightly worse.

Stable Diffusion from scratch

2025 · code

  • Goal. A learning implementation of the Stable Diffusion pipeline, written to understand the architecture and the mathematics behind how noise turns into an image.
  • Approach. I coded everything following Umar Jamil's repository. The main components of the model are the CLIP text encoder, the VAE encoder and decoder, the U-Net denoiser with its attention blocks, and DDPM sampling. Without the compute to train it, I loaded the pretrained v1.5 weights into my implementation.
  • One of my most interesting and insightful projects, where everything I had learnt (architectures, sampling techniques, embedding spaces) came together.

Fourier epicycles from any image

2024 · live demo · code

  • Goal. Most websites that show Fourier epicycles need you to draw the shape by hand or supply an SVG file, and neither works well for real images. I wanted to extend the idea to real images.
  • How it works. Four stages. Edge detection finds the boundaries in the image. A nearest-neighbour traversal orders the scattered edge points into a single connected path. A spline smooths that path and draws the outline of the image. A Fourier transform then turns the path into rotating circles.
  • Limitations. It works reasonably well on cartoons, high-contrast images and portraits, but falls apart on natural images. The fix would be to replace the edge-based approach with a single-stroke tracing algorithm,(which I am looking into).
